# Abdominal Pain Diagnostic Guideline

## Overview

This guideline describes a structured six-step clinical
approach for evaluating patients with abdominal pain.

The workflow is designed to support emergency evaluation,
differential diagnosis, diagnostic testing, and early management.

---

# Step 1 - Initial Assessment

## Objective

Assess hemodynamic stability and identify life-threatening conditions.

---

## Vital Sign Assessment

Evaluate:

- heart rate
- blood pressure
- respiratory rate
- body temperature

Assess whether the patient shows signs of shock.

---

## Signs of Shock

Possible indicators include:

- hypotension
- tachycardia
- altered perfusion

---

## Immediate Resuscitation

If shock is suspected:

- administer intravenous fluids
- provide oxygen support
- initiate vasopressors if necessary

---

## Bedside Diagnostic Tests

Recommended bedside tests:

- pregnancy test for women of reproductive age
- electrocardiogram (ECG)
- capillary blood glucose testing

Purpose:

- detect cardiac abnormalities
- identify diabetes-related emergencies
- detect early pregnancy

---

# Step 2 - History and Physical Examination

## History Taking

Assess pain characteristics:

- location
- onset
- duration
- radiation
- severity
- aggravating factors
- relieving factors

---

## Associated Symptoms

Evaluate for:

- fever
- nausea
- vomiting
- diarrhea
- constipation

---

## Past Medical History

Review:

- prior surgeries
- chronic diseases
- medication history
- gynecologic history
- urinary history

---

## Physical Examination

Perform abdominal examination in sequence:

1. inspection
2. auscultation
3. percussion
4. palpation

---

## Important Findings

Look for:

- localized tenderness
- guarding
- rebound tenderness
- peritonitis signs

---

## Special Examination Maneuvers

Examples:

- Murphy sign
- Carnett sign

---

## Pelvic and Rectal Examination

Important for lower abdominal or pelvic pain.

Consider:

- rectal examination
- pelvic examination in female patients

Potential findings:

- pelvic mass
- occult bleeding
- ectopic pregnancy
- ovarian cysts
- cervical infection

---

# Step 3 - Laboratory Evaluation

## Objective

Use laboratory testing to support or exclude suspected diagnoses.

---

## Basic Laboratory Tests

Recommended baseline investigations:

- complete blood count (CBC)
- C-reactive protein (CRP)
- erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR)
- electrolyte panel
- renal function tests
- blood typing if necessary

---

## Condition-Specific Tests

### Suspected Pancreatitis

Order:

- lipase
- amylase

---

### Suspected Hepatobiliary Disease

Order:

- bilirubin
- liver enzymes

---

### Suspected Bleeding Disorder

Order:

- coagulation studies

---

## Urinalysis

Recommended for patients with suspected:

- urinary tract infection
- kidney stones

Possible findings:

- hematuria
- pyuria

---

## Pregnancy Testing

Mandatory for women with possible pregnancy.

A positive result may influence imaging decisions.

---

## Diagnostic Interpretation

Examples:

- elevated white blood cell count may suggest infection
- elevated bilirubin may suggest biliary obstruction
- hematuria may suggest nephrolithiasis

---

# Step 4 - Imaging Studies

## Objective

Select imaging based on pain location and suspected pathology.

---

## Abdominal Ultrasound

Preferred for:

- gallbladder disease
- hepatobiliary disorders
- pelvic pathology

Advantages:

- non-invasive
- no radiation exposure

---

## CT Abdomen with Contrast

Recommended for suspected:

- appendicitis
- bowel obstruction
- severe pancreatitis
- abdominal abscess

---

## X-ray Imaging

Chest or abdominal X-ray may detect:

- free air under diaphragm
- bowel obstruction
- air-fluid levels

---

## Imaging During Pregnancy

Preferred modalities:

- ultrasound
- MRI if ultrasound is inconclusive

Avoid unnecessary radiation exposure.

---

## Imaging Interpretation

Examples:

### Acute Cholecystitis

Findings:

- gallstones
- gallbladder wall thickening
- right upper quadrant pain

---

### Appendicitis

Findings:

- right lower quadrant pain
- inflammatory bowel wall changes

---

# Step 5 - Diagnostic Integration and Differential Diagnosis

## Objective

Integrate all clinical and diagnostic findings.

---

## Clinical Correlation

History and physical examination remain essential.

Combine:

- symptoms
- physical findings
- laboratory results
- imaging findings

---

## Common Diagnostic Patterns

### Appendicitis

Typical findings:

- RLQ pain
- elevated WBC
- elevated CRP

---

### Acute Cholecystitis

Typical findings:

- RUQ pain
- elevated liver enzymes
- gallstones on ultrasound

---

### Pancreatitis

Typical findings:

- epigastric pain radiating to the back
- elevated lipase or amylase

---

## Additional Diagnostic Testing

If diagnosis remains unclear:

- advanced blood tests
- urine culture
- gastrointestinal endoscopy

---

## Extra-Abdominal Causes

Always consider:

- pneumonia
- myocardial infarction
- abdominal wall disorders

Especially in atypical presentations.

---

## Specialist Consultation

Consult specialists when necessary:

- surgery
- gastroenterology
- obstetrics and gynecology

---

# Step 6 - Initial Management and Disposition

## Objective

Provide stabilization and initiate appropriate treatment.

---

## Supportive Care

May include:

- intravenous fluids
- blood pressure support
- monitoring

---

## Pain Management

Early pain control is recommended.

Evidence suggests analgesia does not interfere with diagnosis.

---

## Antibiotic Therapy

Use broad-spectrum antibiotics if intra-abdominal infection is suspected.

Examples:

- peritonitis
- abdominal abscess

Coverage should include:

- gram-negative organisms
- gram-positive organisms

---

## Surgical Management

Urgent surgical evaluation may be required for:

- appendicitis
- bowel obstruction
- perforated viscus

---

## Monitoring and Disposition

Continue reassessment based on:

- laboratory updates
- imaging results
- clinical progression

Disposition options:

- discharge
- hospital admission
- emergency surgery
